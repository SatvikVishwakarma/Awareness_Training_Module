🔎 What Counts as a Security Incident?

Not every IT problem is a security incident — but many are. Here are the main types you might encounter:

🦠

Malware / Ransomware

Your computer behaves strangely, files are encrypted, or you see a ransom demand. Do not reboot or try to fix it yourself.

🎣

Phishing — Clicked or Opened

You clicked a suspicious link or opened an attachment. Even if nothing visible happened, assume it may have installed something.

🔑

Compromised Credentials

You notice logins you didn't make, receive unexpected MFA codes, or believe your password was exposed in a breach.

📂

Unauthorised Data Leak

Sensitive files were accessed, shared externally, or sent to the wrong person by mistake.

💻

Lost or Stolen Device

A laptop, phone, or USB drive with company data goes missing. Time matters — remote wipe must happen quickly.

👤

Suspicious Insider Activity

A colleague is accessing data they shouldn't, exfiltrating files, or behaving unusually before leaving.

💡 When in doubt, report it. A false alarm costs nothing. A missed incident can cost everything. You will never be blamed for reporting something harmless.

🔄 The Incident Response Lifecycle

Security teams follow a structured process. As an employee, you play a critical role in steps 1 and 2. Click each phase to expand.

1
🔍 Identification
Detect that something is wrong
Your role: You are often the first to notice. Watch for unusual system slowness, unexpected pop-ups, strange login alerts, ransom notes. Report immediately — suspicion is enough.
▶
2
📢 Reporting & Escalation
Notify the right people immediately
Your role: Contact Security via security@yourcompany.com or Slack #security-incidents. Include: what you saw, when, which device, what actions you took. Do NOT discuss in public channels.
▶
3
🔒 Containment
Stop the spread
Your role: Follow security team instructions. Common asks: disconnect Wi-Fi (do NOT power off), lock workstation, stop using the affected account. Don't attempt to clean up malware yourself — you may destroy forensic evidence.
▶
4
🧹 Eradication
Remove the threat — handled by security and IT
Your role: Cooperate with IT. You may receive a loaner device. Provide timeline details: exactly what you clicked, what you typed, when it happened.
▶
5
🔧 Recovery
Restore normal operations safely
Your role: Reset passwords and MFA as instructed. Verify your restored system before resuming. Alert IT if anything still seems wrong.
▶
6
📋 Lessons Learned
Post-incident review to prevent recurrence
Your role: Participate openly in any post-incident debrief. This is blameless — the goal is to improve processes, not assign blame. Your honest account is invaluable.
▶

📋 Your Quick-Reference Card

📢

Step 1: Report Immediately

Contact security@yourcompany.com or #security-incidents. Include: what, when, which device, what you did.

🔌

Step 2: Disconnect — Don't Power Off

Disable Wi-Fi or unplug the cable. Powering off destroys forensic evidence and can worsen ransomware.

🤐

Step 3: Need-to-Know Only

Don't post in general Slack or email threads. The security team controls all communications.

📸

Step 4: Preserve Evidence

Don't delete suspicious emails. Take a photo of any ransom screen. Note exact times and actions.

🤝

Step 5: Cooperate Fully

Follow instructions from IT and Security. Provide your device when asked. Be honest — no blame, only learning.

🔒

Step 6: Reset & Verify

Change all passwords after the all-clear. Enable MFA everywhere. Confirm your device is clean before resuming.

Report incidents to security@yourcompany.com · Emergency: #security-incidents
