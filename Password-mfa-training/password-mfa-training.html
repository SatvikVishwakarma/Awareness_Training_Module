Why Do Passwords Get Compromised?

🎯

Credential Stuffing

Attackers take leaked username/password pairs from one site and try them on every other site. If you reuse passwords, one breach exposes everything.

🔨

Brute Force & Dictionary

Automated tools guess billions of common passwords and variations per second. "Password1!" falls in under a second.

🎣

Phishing & Keyloggers

Malicious sites or software capture your password as you type it — strength doesn't matter if it's stolen in real time.

🌐

Data Breaches

Over 15 billion credentials are floating on the dark web. Websites you trusted have been hacked — your password may already be exposed.

⚠️ 81% of hacking-related breaches are caused by weak or stolen passwords (Verizon DBIR). The simplest improvement: stop reusing passwords.

📲 Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA adds a second verification step beyond your password. But not all MFA is created equal.

Method How It Works Phish-Resistant? Security Level
📱 SMS / Text Code 6-digit code sent via text message ❌ No — SIM-swappable, interceptable ⚠️ Low
📧 Email Code Code sent to your inbox ❌ No — if email is compromised, so is this ⚠️ Low
🔢 Authenticator App (TOTP) Time-based codes (Google Auth, Authy, etc.) ⚠️ Partial — can be phished in real time 🔶 Medium
✅ Push Notification Tap Approve on your phone ⚠️ Partial — vulnerable to push bombing 🔶 Medium
🔑 Hardware Key (FIDO2) Physical YubiKey or passkey ✅ Yes — cryptographically bound to domain ✅ High
🪪 Passkeys (WebAuthn) Biometric or device-bound credential ✅ Yes — no shared secret to steal ✅ High

💡 Company rule of thumb: Use your authenticator app at minimum. Use a hardware key or passkey for privileged access. Never share any MFA code with anyone — even IT.

📵 SIM Swapping: Why SMS MFA Is Risky

A SIM swap attack tricks your mobile carrier into transferring your phone number to a SIM card the attacker controls.

1️⃣

Attacker Researches You

Finds your name, phone number, and carrier from social media or data breaches.

2️⃣

Calls Your Carrier

Impersonates you, provides answers to security questions (from your social media), claims lost phone.

3️⃣

Carrier Swaps SIM

Your number now routes to the attacker's device. Your phone shows "No Service."

4️⃣

Attacker Owns Your Accounts

All SMS codes and calls go to them. They reset passwords and take over accounts.

🛡️ How to protect yourself:

Switch from SMS MFA to an authenticator app or hardware key wherever possible. Set a carrier PIN/passcode at your mobile provider. Never post your phone number or carrier publicly. If your phone suddenly shows "No Service," call your carrier immediately from another phone.

📋 Your Key Takeaways

🔑

Use Strong, Unique Passwords

12+ characters, unique per site. Passphrases like sky-lamp-river-77! are easier to remember and very hard to crack.

🏦

Get a Password Manager

1Password, Bitwarden, or your company-approved tool. Never reuse passwords across sites.

📲

Use the Right MFA

Authenticator app > SMS. Hardware key / Passkey > everything else. Never share any MFA code with anyone.

🚨

Unexpected Push- Report It!

Deny all pushes you didn't trigger, change your password immediately, and report to your security team.

📵

Watch for SIM Swaps

Sudden "No Service"- Call your carrier from another phone. Set a carrier PIN. Move off SMS MFA.
